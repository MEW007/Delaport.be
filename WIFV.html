iv. A real example of board-level governance and capital allocation

Building a JV from scratch, then governing it.

Denys · IJmuiden Ver onshore HV grid connection · TenneT
JV board member across three partners: contract structure, equity funding and capital allocation
Denys · Van Vulpen · Alsema · Onshore HV infrastructure · Dutch grid operator TenneT

The structure

To tender and execute the IJmuiden Ver onshore HV grid connection for TenneT, Denys formed a three-party joint venture with Van Vulpen and Alsema. I represented Denys as board member of the JV, alongside Legal and the CFOs of the partner companies, and was responsible for structuring the inter-partner contractual framework.

The governance

At board level I was involved in equity funding decisions, equipment and resource allocation across three independent organisations, and logistics coordination for a complex onshore HV execution programme. The JV required genuine capital governance: decisions had legal, financial and operational consequences for all three partners simultaneously.

The financial reporting

I ran the internal P&L of the JV and oversaw the required public financial statements. This meant maintaining full financial visibility across partner contributions, cost allocations and reporting obligations, and presenting a coherent financial picture to the board throughout the project lifecycle.

The relevance to WIFV

A JV between three partners is structurally similar to an investment vehicle with multiple stakeholders: equity must be allocated, governance frameworks must hold under pressure, and financial reporting must satisfy both internal decision-makers and external parties.

Governing capital across multiple partners requires more than financial modeling. It requires the credibility to make allocation decisions that three independent boards will accept. That is the experience I bring to WIFV.
I would welcome a conversation about how this profile could serve WIFV’s mandate, not as a substitute for deep project-finance experience, but as the executor’s seat beside it.
