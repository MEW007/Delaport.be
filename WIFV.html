iv. A real example of analytical judgment under pressure

When the contract says no, the analysis finds a way.

Jan De Nul · Offshore wind · 2022
A €9.6M cost claim recovered on a fixed-price EPC contract
Ukraine war price shock · Labour, equipment & fuel indices · Near-full recovery

The situation

In February 2022, the war in Ukraine triggered severe inflation across labour, equipment and fuel markets. Our contract was fixed-price EPC with no reimbursement mechanism. On paper, we carried the full cost exposure with no contractual route to recovery.

The analysis

I rebuilt the complete project cost structure using actual market price indices, separating the impact across three categories: labour, equipment and fuel. The output was a detailed, watertight Excel model tied directly to the contractual selling price breakdown, leaving no room for the client to dispute the methodology.

The process

The claim was presented through graduated communication with the client, building the case incrementally rather than opening with the full number. Internally, I maintained regular alignment with the Project Director throughout, who joined the final negotiation meeting to support the close.

The outcome

Near-full recovery of the €9.6M claim. The index mechanism I developed was subsequently incorporated into new contract templates, changing how the company structures fixed-price exposure going forward.

A fixed-price contract is not the end of the conversation. Rigorous analysis, presented with discipline, can recover value that the contract appears to have closed off. That is the lens I bring to an investment portfolio.
I would welcome a conversation about how this profile could serve WIFV’s mandate, not as a substitute for deep project-finance experience, but as the executor’s seat beside it.
